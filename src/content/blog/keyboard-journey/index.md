I didn't plan to write about keyboards. But after years of buying, selling, and rebuilding, one self-silenced Topre, and a charity rescue, I think I've finally reached the end.

This is the story of how I went from chasing "thock" to loving "clack," from hype switches to OG Cherry MX, and from a drawer full of boards to a two-keyboard rotation with three distinct builds that I genuinely believe is my endgame.

## The layout evolution

My journey started in 2022 with the IrisLab Jris65. Then I stepped away from keyboards entirely for three years. I was an outsider again.

I came back in 2025 with the ATK V75X, then cycled through 75% boards, back to 65%, then 60%, and finally landed on the True HHKB layout.

**65% → 70% → 75% → 65% → 60% → True HHKB**

Each step was a refinement. The early boards taught me the basics. The 75% phase introduced me to copper weights and denser cases. The return to 65% and then 60% made me realize I didn't need arrow keys or a function row. And the HHKB layout was the final form: everything I need, nothing I don't.

## The switch hype cycle

I joined the hype train early. I waited months for the "next big thing" linear switch, paid premiums, and sold them at a loss when the next one dropped.

Then I got tired. The new switches felt identical to the old ones. I realized I was chasing a ghost.

So I went back to the foundation: **OG Cherry MX**. MX2A Blacks for linear, MX2A Browns for tactile. No hype, no waiting, no group buys. Just switches that have been refined for decades and still feel better than most of what's on the market.

## The sound preference shift

**Thocky > Clacky** was my starting point.

**Clacky > Thocky** is where I ended up.

The shift happened somewhere between the RD75 and the Neo65 Cu. I wanted sharp, high-pitched, energetic, firm, and crisp. Not deep, muted, or foamy.

This shift is what eventually led me to my endgame configurations.

## The endgame rotation

Endgame is not one board. It's a set of builds that cover your entire preference spectrum. Mine covers three distinct experiences across two chassis.

### 1. Alas60 — The Mechanical Keyboard

I have two builds on the same Alas60 chassis. Same case, same PCB, completely different souls.

#### Build 1: The Reference

**Config:** Aluminum plate + O-ring + MX2A Blacks + GMK Verdant Retro.

This is my reference. High-pitched, sharp, full, energetic. Firm, crisp, controlled. It does exactly what I want a mechanical keyboard to do.

#### Build 2: The Contrast

**Config:** Full ABS plate + firmer O-ring + MX2A Browns + GMK Verdant Retro.

I built this as a contrast to Build 1. It taught me that ABS is a natural damper — you need a firmer O-ring to compensate. The result is a warm, mellow, mechanical tactile experience that still retains crispness.

#### Why two builds?

Because one board can't cover everything. Build 1 is aggressive and sharp. Build 2 is warm and relaxed. I swap between them depending on my mood. Same chassis, two different typing experiences. It's like having two keyboards in one.

### 2. FXXX EC — The Electro-Capacitive Keyboard

**Config:** OF Studio FXXX EC case + EM60v2 PCB + MetaPulse EC kit + RS 45g domes + PBTFans Lyla.

The EC tactile experience is unmatched. Smooth, buttery, bouncy, full-dome collapse with a "snow-crunch" sound. It's the tactile endgame.

## The 2026 acquisition

The Alas60 was a 2023 group buy. By 2026, it was long sold out and considered rare.

Then something strange happened. **Acid & Co.**, the vendor, discovered **3-year untouched Alas stock** in their warehouse. The original owner had never claimed it.

The vendor and the original owner made a deal: rather than sell it on the aftermarket, they would **charity the board**.

I acquired it in **BNIB (Brand New In Box), A-Stock condition**. My payment was donated to charity under three names: the original owner, Acid & Co., and myself.

It wasn't just a purchase. It was a community-driven rescue from warehouse purgatory. A clean, positive origin story for my endgame board.

## The fine print

This is not a build log. It's a story about taste, switches, and letting go — but not in a toxic way. I'm grateful for the hobby. I'm grateful for the boards, the switches, the late nights, and the friends I made.

If you're still chasing, that's great. I hope you find your endgame too. Just don't forget where you came from. Don't be a _kacang lupa kulit_.

The endgame is quiet, peaceful, and full of gratitude.

[^1]: "Thock" is that deep, muted, bassy keyboard sound. "Clack" is sharp, high-pitched, and loud. I started wanting thock. I ended up loving clack. With ABS keycaps and Cherry MX switches on a foamless board, you get that crisp, snappy clack every time you type. Pure clacky supremacy.
